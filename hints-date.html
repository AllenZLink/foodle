How the archive works

Every entry is the official NYT answer for that date. Each card is collapsed by default — tap once to reveal the answer, tap again to reveal the category and definition. Reveals are stored locally in your browser so you don't accidentally spoil a date you wanted to attempt yourself.

Why look up old Wordles?

  • To settle a bet. "What was Wordle on our anniversary?" Now you can prove it.
  • To study patterns. Curious whether the NYT favors -OUND endings or double-Ls? Filter by starting letter and read the room.
  • To replay missed days. If you broke a streak, going back and solving the missed day on your own (without scrolling to the answer) can be its own small ritual.
  • To win trivia night. Honestly: most of the time, this.

Frequently asked

How far back does the archive go?

To Wordle's public launch in October 2021. The list above is a representative sample for this prototype; the production archive includes every published answer.

Why don't I see a particular date?

The NYT has occasionally swapped or skipped puzzles for editorial reasons — sensitivity, current events, holidays. We mirror their official log, so anything they pulled won't appear here.

Are the answers sourced from the NYT?

The answers are public-knowledge facts about a specific calendar day's puzzle. We don't republish NYT editorial copy — the hints, categories, and notes are written by us.

Can I clear the reveals on my browser?

Yes — your reveal history lives in localStorage and never leaves your device. Clearing site data in your browser will reset everything.
